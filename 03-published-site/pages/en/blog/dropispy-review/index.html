The verdict

Dropispy is a powerful tool for one job: spying on Meta ads tied to Shopify stores. Its focus on dropshipping links each creative to a real seller, where generic ad libraries stop at the image. That is what makes its comprehensive data useful.

As an alternative to $149 platforms it wins on price. Outside that lane, you pay for a database you will only half use.

What Dropispy actually is

An adspy tool built for dropshipping that collects thousands of commerce ads daily from Facebook and Instagram, then connects each one to the product and the store behind it. The company is Swiss, based in Geneva, specialised in data collection and scraping.

That ad-to-product-to-store link connects three data layers, and it is the capability that separates this tool from a plain ad library. Most libraries show you an isolated creative. Dropispy shows you the creative, the product page behind it and the store running the campaign.

The adspy database

The core module gives access to 50M+ ads enriched with social proof: likes, shares and comments. That is the feature people buy the tool for. You search by keyword and filter by creation date, media type, country, language and interaction level. Six filters that cover most niche research.

Demographic filters go down to gender and target age range. On a tool priced at $30, that level of detailed targeting data stays rare in this category.

Search runs on keywords, not on advertiser ID. You cannot track one Facebook page and pull its full advertising history. Advanced users name this as the biggest workflow gap in the platform.

Shop spy and the store ranking

Dropispy claims the only complete ranking of the best ecommerce and dropshipping stores worldwide, backed by a database of 400,000 stores.

Open a store profile and you see its hot products right now, which makes niche discovery far faster than manual browsing. The traffic ranking tells you which stores deserve a closer look.

This is the feature that justifies the subscription for most dropshippers spying on a specific brand or niche. Spotting an ad that performs is one thing. Tracing it back to the store, reading the catalogue and analyzing the competitor’s best sellers by sales rank is another.

Shop spy sits behind the paid plans. On the free tier you never touch it.

The Chrome extension

The extension overlays data directly on your feeds and on the product pages you visit. Ad run duration, apps installed on the store, domain age. Passive spying while you browse.

It works from the free plan, which makes it the cleanest entry point for testing the tool without paying anything. Its known weakness: it breaks after Chrome updates and the fix sometimes takes weeks.

The filters that change your results

Out of roughly ten filters, four do 80% of the work. The rest refine a search that already returns clean results. Learning which is which is the whole learning curve of this tool. Every other feature sits on top of those four.

The country filter deserves attention. The US market leads most consumer markets by two to four months, so trends land there first. Filter on the US, spot what is climbing, prepare your local launch before the trend lands.

The interaction filter traps beginners. Sorting by highest engagement surfaces advertisers spending six figures a month. You will not compete with them on a testing budget.

Work the middle band instead, roughly 500 to 5,000 interactions. Those campaigns belong to operators your size, on products still accessible through normal supplier channels. That single choice makes your research far more effective.

The most effective daily combination: video, last 30 days, your target country, sorted by interactions, then a manual read of the top twenty. Budget fifteen minutes per session. Simple, repeatable and effective.

What the free plan really hides

The free plan gives unlimited credits but locks the last two months of ads. You browse old inventory only.

For a dropshipper that is disqualifying. A product spotted on a three-month-old ad is already saturated or already dead. The free tier teaches you the interface. It does not find you a winning product, and it will not support a real market study.

No card is required at signup, just an email address to verify. There is no free trial of Premium, only this permanent free plan.

The credit system decoded

Every action burns credits on a published scale: 30 to display a list of ads, 10 to open an ad detail or a Shopify view, 15 to list domains.

Run the maths. The 150,000 Premium credits buy 5,000 ad listings a month, about 166 a day. An effective research session consumes 300 to 500 credits.

That leaves you around 350 sessions a month on Premium. Well above what a solo seller testing three to five products needs.

Business multiplies credits by twelve for a price multiplied by 8.4. The per-credit cost drops, provided your team actually consumes that volume. Below roughly 4,000 sessions a month you overpay.

Do Dropispy promo codes mean anything

The most circulated discount advertises 50% off Premium at $14.90 a month. That is not a promo code. It is the official annual rate published on the pricing page.

A second offer runs at 30% off for three months. That one is real but short. It does not change the arithmetic on a subscription you will keep six months or more.

Keep one principle for this market. When an offer holds above 40% permanently, it is structural pricing rather than a genuine deal. Compare against the annual price before hunting for codes.

Dropispy also runs an affiliate program available on request through email. That explains the volume of glowing reviews you meet when you search the brand name, so read them with that filter on.

How to use Dropispy for product research

The method that returns the most results for the fewest credits runs in five steps.

  1. Filter by target country and creation date over the last 30 days.
  2. Sort by interaction count to isolate creatives that drive engagement.
  3. Open details on ads running longer than three weeks.
  4. Connect the ad back to the store through shop spy and read the full catalogue.
  5. Log the product, its selling price and the advertising angle used.

Step three carries the weight. A long run time is the strongest signal in the whole dataset, because an ad still live after three weeks is profitable. Nobody burns budget for a month on a losing campaign.

The classic error is stopping at the like count on a viral post. A creative at 40,000 reactions on a $15 product is usually saturated already. Reaction volume is a weak signal on its own. Look for mid-engagement ads with long run times instead.

Once you have the product, sourcing, pricing and store build remain. Dropispy stops at detection. That boundary defines the tool and explains why most users run it alongside a second platform.

Turning ad data into product validation

Validation is where most dropshippers waste their subscription. They collect ideas and never build a repeatable scoring process from the data sitting in front of them.

Four signals carry real predictive weight when you study a creative:

  • Run duration past three weeks, the most effective and least gameable profitability proxy.
  • The same product surfacing across three or more independent sellers.
  • Comment sentiment on the ad post showing purchase intent rather than friend tagging.
  • A store whose traffic and sales trend climbs while the campaign stays live, the slowest signal to read but the most reliable.

Run duration works because ad spend is self-policing. No operator funds a campaign with no sales for a month. These spending patterns beat any engagement count you can sort on.

Comment reading is the step nobody does. Open the post, read forty comments and separate "where do I buy this" from "look at this". The first group signals sales. The second signals virality without conversion.

Cross-seller validation protects you from one-off winners. One store scaling a product proves that store's marketing strategies work. Three independent stores that sell it prove the market wants it.

What the platform will not give you is the sales figure itself. It shows the advertising, the store and the catalogue. Revenue, conversion rate and margin are never visible.

Score your ideas on those four signals before you spend on testing. Ten minutes of study saves a $300 ad test, and the habit compounds across a niche.

Three concrete scenarios

Finding a winning product in an unfamiliar niche

You target the pet niche without ever having sold there. Filter your country, video, last 30 days, broad keyword like "dog" or "cat".

You get a list of live ads. Open the ones running three weeks or more, trace back to the stores through shop spy and note the products appearing across several independent competitors. Two sessions give you ten ideas to study.

A product pushed by three separate sellers at once signals real demand rather than one lucky campaign. In dropshipping that is the strongest anti-flop filter available.

Reverse-engineering a competitor store

You already know a competitor that performs. Search the competitor domain, open the store profile, list the products it pushes.

The Chrome extension completes the picture as you browse the site: Shopify apps installed, tech stack, domain age and run duration of active campaigns.

What you will not see: revenue, traffic volume, acquisition cost and margins. Dropispy shows the storefront and the advertising, never the accounting.

Building your creative angle library

Before filming your own videos, gather twenty to thirty competitor creatives on your product. Log the first-three-second hook, the core promise and the call-to-action format for each. That library becomes your content bank.

Without bulk export you download media one file at a time and analyze each hook by hand. That is the ugliest part of the workflow.

Where the tool falls over

You move onto TikTok Shop. Dropispy sees nothing there, not the creatives, not the sellers, not the products taking off. Same story for WooCommerce sellers running paid social outside Meta.

At that point you swap tools or add a second tool with wider network coverage. Both options cost money, so decide before your quota resets. Our comparison of the best ad spy tools covers the options network by network.

The mistakes that burn your credits

Three habits drain a monthly quota without producing a single usable product.

The first is chaining result lists with no precise filter. Each display costs 30 credits. Twenty vague searches in one session evaporate for nothing.

The second is opening every ad on a page. At 10 credits per detailed view, reading thirty results costs more than the search itself. Sort visually before you click, and explore the shortlist only.

The third is rerunning the same query weekly without logging previous results. You pay twice for the same information. Keep a file listing rejected products and the reason for each rejection.

One habit halves consumption. Prepare queries outside the tool, list your three keywords and your date window, then execute. Blind browsing is what costs the most.

What's missing from Dropispy

Five blind spots come up across advanced user feedback:

  • No bulk export of results into a spreadsheet or swipe file.
  • No advertiser-first search feature, only keywords and domains.
  • No ad spend or traffic estimation, unlike AdSpy.
  • No batch actions such as tagging, collection building or bulk creative sorting.
  • No sourcing or fulfillment layer, so supplier and fulfillment work happens elsewhere.

API access is listed on the Business plan but public documentation is absent. If your goal is feeding a dashboard or an automation pipeline, confirm the terms with support before committing to $249.90.

The export gap weighs more than it looks day to day. You find fifteen relevant creatives and you process them one by one inside the interface. On a weekly research rhythm that is hours lost.

What users say

Trustpilot reviews stay thin, around forty, with a high overall score. Three themes repeat in the positive feedback.

  • Support speed and tone, reachable through live chat.
  • Interface described as intuitive, with a short learning curve for beginners.
  • Value for money against tools priced above $100, with regular seasonal deals.

On the negative side, criticism lands on two points. One user challenged data freshness and the team replied publicly confirming an incident since fixed. A few billing disputes also appear.

Feature requests repeat across those experiences: bulk export, TikTok coverage and deeper history. The team answers publicly on the review platform, which is a fair trust signal on a tool sold across multiple countries.

Weigh the sample size. Forty reviews is not representative, especially on a platform running an active affiliate program.

Treat published testimonials and affiliate recommendations as marketing. Judge effectiveness on your own test.

Who Dropispy is right for

Take Dropispy if you tick these boxes: you sell on Meta, competitors sell on Shopify, ad spend under $1,000 a month, one operator. That profile fits the tool perfectly.

Skip it if you run a multi-client agency, if TikTok carries weight in your mix or if you need to export data into another tool. In that case a multi-platform choice serves you better.

The tipping point arrives fast. The moment you go multi-network or push past $1,000 monthly, the tool's ceiling becomes the limiting factor in your acquisition strategy.

FAQ

Is Dropispy worth the price

Yes for a solo dropshipper who sells on Meta. At $29.90 you access 50 million ads, engagement metrics and the store analysis layer. AdSpy charges five times more for comparable scope.

Dropispy or AdSpy, which should you pick

AdSpy if you need the deepest Meta index and domain-level search, with $149 monthly to absorb. Dropispy is the better choice if budget matters and shop spy fits your research workflow.

Does Dropispy have an API

API access is listed on the Business plan at $249.90. Public documentation is not available, so confirm the terms with support before committing at that level.

How far back does the data go

Independent testing rarely surfaces ads past three months. For studying how a creative trend evolved across a year, this tool will not carry you.

Does Dropispy cover TikTok

No. Coverage stops at Facebook and Instagram. No TikTok, YouTube, Google or Pinterest data is indexed.

Can the free plan find winning products

Barely. The last two months of ads are hidden and shop spy is locked. A dropshipper can explore the interface, not run comprehensive product discovery.

Are Dropispy promo codes real

The $14.90 offer marketed as 50% off is the official annual rate. A genuine 30% discount runs for three months. Compare against annual pricing before hunting codes.

How does credit rollover work

Unused credits carry into the next month on the Business plan. You can also add top-ups in blocks of 50,000 or 100,000 credits if your quota runs dry mid-month.

How good is Dropispy customer support

Live chat is a paid-plan feature and the team also answers by email. User feedback rates response speed highly, which is not a given at this price level.

Is Dropispy essential for finding winning products

No tool is essential. It is one effective source of market signals among several, and it works best combined with a supplier check and a real ad test.

Copyfy vs Dropispy, what is the difference
